# Escrow

Escrow in blockchain technology refers to a mechanism where digital assets are locked in a smart contract and released to parties only when predefined conditions are met. Unlike traditional escrow managed by a centralized third party, blockchain escrow is governed by code. The smart contract acts as an impartial agent, automatically verifying input data—such as oracle feeds or transaction confirmations—and executing the transfer of funds or assets without the risk of counterparty default or human bias.

## Definition

Escrow in blockchain technology refers to a mechanism where digital assets are locked in a smart contract and released to parties only when predefined conditions are met. Unlike traditional escrow managed by a centralized third party, blockchain escrow is governed by code. The smart contract acts as an impartial agent, automatically verifying input data—such as oracle feeds or transaction confirmations—and executing the transfer of funds or assets without the risk of counterparty default or human bias.

## Simple explanation

Imagine you want to buy a digital item from someone you don't know. Instead of sending them money and hoping they send the item, you both use an 'escrow' smart contract. You put your money into this digital vault, and the seller puts the item in as well. The vault only opens and gives the money to the seller if the item arrives, or gives your money back if something goes wrong. It acts like an automated, fair referee.

## Why it matters

Blockchain escrow eliminates the need for trusted third-party intermediaries, significantly reducing costs and risks in peer-to-peer transactions. It fosters trust in global trade, online marketplaces, and complex DeFi protocols by ensuring neither party can act dishonestly.

## How it works

The parties involved agree on the terms of the escrow, which are encoded into a smart contract. Buyers deposit assets into the contract, which holds them in custody. The contract monitors for specific triggering events, such as a confirmation from a delivery service or a price target, and automatically distributes the assets according to the logic programmed upon success or failure.

## Real-world example

The decentralized marketplace OpenSea uses escrow-like smart contracts to hold NFT assets and payment funds until the exchange transaction is successfully verified.

## Advantages

- Eliminates reliance on costly intermediaries
- Provides verifiable trust between anonymous parties
- Automated execution minimizes human error

## Limitations

- Vulnerable to flaws in smart contract code
- Difficult to resolve complex subjective disputes
- Requires precise predefined conditions to work

## Common misconceptions

- Some believe blockchain escrow is always reversible, but it is typically final once conditions are triggered.
- People think that all escrow requires a human manager, whereas blockchain escrow is entirely automated by code.
