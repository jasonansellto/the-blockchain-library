# TPS

TPS stands for Transactions Per Second, a performance metric used to measure the throughput of a blockchain network. It represents the number of valid transactions a network can process and record to its ledger within a single second. Because blockchain throughput is influenced by block size, block time, and consensus mechanisms, TPS serves as a primary benchmark for comparing the scalability of different distributed ledger technologies and evaluating their suitability for high-frequency or global-scale applications.

## Definition

TPS stands for Transactions Per Second, a performance metric used to measure the throughput of a blockchain network. It represents the number of valid transactions a network can process and record to its ledger within a single second. Because blockchain throughput is influenced by block size, block time, and consensus mechanisms, TPS serves as a primary benchmark for comparing the scalability of different distributed ledger technologies and evaluating their suitability for high-frequency or global-scale applications.

## Simple explanation

Think of TPS like the speed of a highway toll booth. If the toll booth can only process one car per second, there will be a traffic jam. A higher TPS means more cars can pass through the gate at once, which makes the network faster and capable of handling more people at the same time.

## Why it matters

TPS is a critical indicator of a blockchain's potential for mainstream adoption and its ability to compete with centralized financial networks like Visa. If a network has low TPS, it will experience high fees and delays during periods of high usage, which hinders its utility for everyday retail transactions.

## How it works

TPS is calculated by dividing the total number of transactions successfully included in a block by the duration of the block time. For instance, if a block contains 2,000 transactions and is produced every 10 seconds, the average throughput is 200 TPS. Many modern networks use parallel processing or layer-2 solutions to increase this throughput.

## Real-world example

While Bitcoin typically operates at roughly 7 TPS, high-performance blockchains like Solana advertise theoretical capacities of over 50,000 TPS to support complex decentralized applications.

## Advantages

- Provides a clear benchmark for network speed
- Helps developers choose the right blockchain for their needs
- Highlights the scalability progress of a project

## Limitations

- Often ignores latency and finality times
- High TPS can lead to network centralization
- Theoretical maximums rarely reflect real-world performance

## Common misconceptions

- Many people assume higher TPS always means a better blockchain, but this often requires sacrificing decentralization.
- TPS is frequently confused with latency, which measures the time a single transaction takes to confirm rather than total throughput.
