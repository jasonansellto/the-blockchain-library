# KYC

KYC, or Know Your Customer, refers to the mandatory process employed by financial institutions and cryptocurrency service providers to verify the identity of their clients. This regulatory framework requires entities to collect verifiable personal documentation, such as government-issued IDs, proof of address, and sometimes biometric data, to mitigate risks associated with money laundering, terrorism financing, and fraud. In the blockchain sector, KYC is the bridge between pseudonymous digital assets and the traditional legal financial system, ensuring that service providers remain compliant with global anti-money laundering (AML) regulations.

## Definition

KYC, or Know Your Customer, refers to the mandatory process employed by financial institutions and cryptocurrency service providers to verify the identity of their clients. This regulatory framework requires entities to collect verifiable personal documentation, such as government-issued IDs, proof of address, and sometimes biometric data, to mitigate risks associated with money laundering, terrorism financing, and fraud. In the blockchain sector, KYC is the bridge between pseudonymous digital assets and the traditional legal financial system, ensuring that service providers remain compliant with global anti-money laundering (AML) regulations.

## Simple explanation

Imagine walking into a bank to open an account; they won't let you just hand over money without seeing your ID first. KYC is exactly that, but for the digital world. It is the digital equivalent of showing your passport at a border. It helps apps and websites prove you are a real person and not someone hiding their identity to break the law, which keeps the whole system safer for everyone.

## Why it matters

KYC serves as the critical regulatory layer that allows blockchain projects to operate within the global legal framework. By requiring identity verification, it prevents illicit actors from exploiting crypto services and provides the institutional legitimacy necessary for wider mass adoption.

## How it works

The process begins when a user submits personal documents or biometric data through a secure portal. The platform then uses automated software or manual reviewers to cross-reference this information against government databases and watchlists. Once the identity is successfully confirmed and vetted for risk, the user is granted access to the platform's full suite of services, such as high-volume trading or fiat withdrawals.

## Real-world example

Major centralized exchanges like Coinbase or Kraken require users to complete a multi-tier KYC verification process before allowing them to deposit fiat currency or trade specific high-value assets.

## Advantages

- Reduces platform risk of illegal activity
- Increases trust for institutional investors
- Ensures compliance with national laws
- Protects against identity theft and fraud

## Limitations

- Increases user onboarding friction
- Centralizes sensitive personal data
- Potential for data breaches
- Excludes unbanked populations globally

## Common misconceptions

- Many people believe KYC makes the blockchain itself less private, though it only impacts the centralized exchange interface.
- Some users incorrectly assume that KYC is a requirement for using decentralized protocols like self-custody wallets, which is not true.
