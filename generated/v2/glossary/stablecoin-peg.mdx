# Stablecoin Peg

The stablecoin peg is the target value that a stablecoin aims to maintain, usually expressed as a 1:1 parity with a specific fiat currency like the US dollar. Maintaining the peg is the primary function of a stablecoin project, utilizing various mechanisms such as arbitrage, collateral adjustment, or supply-demand modification. When a coin loses its peg—known as 'de-pegging'—the token's value deviates from the target asset, which can lead to significant market volatility and loss of investor confidence.

## Definition

The stablecoin peg is the target value that a stablecoin aims to maintain, usually expressed as a 1:1 parity with a specific fiat currency like the US dollar. Maintaining the peg is the primary function of a stablecoin project, utilizing various mechanisms such as arbitrage, collateral adjustment, or supply-demand modification. When a coin loses its peg—known as 'de-pegging'—the token's value deviates from the target asset, which can lead to significant market volatility and loss of investor confidence.

## Simple explanation

A peg is like a tether that keeps a boat from drifting away. The stablecoin is the boat, and the US dollar is the dock. The 'peg' is the rope that keeps the boat's price stuck to the dollar's value so it does not float away into price changes.

## Why it matters

The entire utility of a stablecoin depends on its ability to stay pegged; if the peg breaks, it fails its purpose as a reliable store of value or medium of exchange. A stable peg is the prerequisite for trust in the broader digital asset economy.

## How it works

Market participants, specifically arbitrageurs, monitor the stablecoin price. If the price rises above the peg, they sell the token for profit until the price drops. If it falls below, they buy the token for cheap, effectively driving the price back up toward the target.

## Real-world example

The de-pegging of UST in May 2022.

## Advantages

- Provides predictability for transactions
- Essential for financial planning
- Allows for stable pricing in commerce

## Limitations

- Vulnerable to extreme market events
- Peg maintenance is often costly
- Psychological reliance on the issuer

## Common misconceptions

- People often assume the peg is guaranteed by law. Some think de-pegging is impossible for large coins.
