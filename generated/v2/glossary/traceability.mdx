# Traceability

Traceability on a blockchain is the capability to track the complete history, location, and condition of an item from its origin through every step of its lifecycle. It relies on the immutability of the distributed ledger, which ensures that record-keeping cannot be falsified or deleted after entry. This creates a robust audit trail, critical for industries where safety, authenticity, and regulatory adherence are paramount, enabling stakeholders to respond quickly to recalls and supply chain bottlenecks.

## Definition

Traceability on a blockchain is the capability to track the complete history, location, and condition of an item from its origin through every step of its lifecycle. It relies on the immutability of the distributed ledger, which ensures that record-keeping cannot be falsified or deleted after entry. This creates a robust audit trail, critical for industries where safety, authenticity, and regulatory adherence are paramount, enabling stakeholders to respond quickly to recalls and supply chain bottlenecks.

## Simple explanation

Traceability is like having a rewind button for a product's history. By scanning a code, you can see exactly where the product was grown, which factory touched it, and how it was shipped, ensuring it arrived safely and legally at your doorstep.

## Why it matters

In sectors like pharmaceuticals, food, and high-end retail, traceability is a matter of safety and trust. It empowers consumers to make informed choices and helps businesses maintain quality standards across global operations.

## How it works

Sensors and scanners record data at every point in a production cycle, which is pushed directly to the blockchain. Each piece of data is linked to the previous entry, creating a permanent, sequential chain of information. If a product is tampered with or fails a check, the history remains visible to everyone in the authorized network.

## Real-world example

Walmart uses blockchain to track the origins of leafy greens, reducing the time needed to trace the source of foodborne illnesses from days to seconds.

## Advantages

- Rapid identification of contaminated products
- Verified proof of authenticity
- Improved consumer and partner trust

## Limitations

- Reliance on accurate data at point-of-origin
- High costs of hardware integration
- Privacy concerns with public ledger data

## Common misconceptions

- Blockchain provides 100% data accuracy.
- Traceability is synonymous with total transparency.
