# Network Congestion

Network congestion occurs when the number of transactions being broadcast to a blockchain exceeds the network's processing capacity. Because blocks have limited size and throughput speed, an overflow of pending transactions results in a backlog in the mempool. This phenomenon typically forces users to pay higher priority fees to ensure faster confirmation, effectively pricing out users who opt for standard or lower fee tiers during periods of extreme high demand.

## Definition

Network congestion occurs when the number of transactions being broadcast to a blockchain exceeds the network's processing capacity. Because blocks have limited size and throughput speed, an overflow of pending transactions results in a backlog in the mempool. This phenomenon typically forces users to pay higher priority fees to ensure faster confirmation, effectively pricing out users who opt for standard or lower fee tiers during periods of extreme high demand.

## Simple explanation

Think of a highway during rush hour. When too many cars try to use the road at once, a traffic jam forms. On a blockchain, these 'traffic jams' mean your transaction takes much longer to process, and you have to pay a higher 'toll' to get to the front of the line.

## Why it matters

Network congestion directly impacts the usability and cost-efficiency of a blockchain. Understanding it explains why transaction fees spike during popular periods, such as NFT drops or high market volatility, affecting overall user experience.

## How it works

Transactions are sent to a 'mempool' where they wait for miners or validators to include them in the next block. When the network is congested, validators prioritize transactions with the highest gas fees. As the backlog grows, confirmation times increase significantly for anyone not paying a premium fee.

## Real-world example

During the peak of Ethereum's DeFi summer, the network experienced severe congestion, causing transaction fees to exceed $100 for simple token swaps.

## Advantages

- Demonstrates high network demand
- Incentivizes network throughput upgrades
- Allows miners to earn more fees

## Limitations

- Significantly increases transaction costs
- Slows down decentralized application performance
- Deters casual and small-scale users

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe congestion is a sign of a broken network, but it often reflects high adoption and utility.
- Users often think congestion is caused by system bugs, when it is actually a natural result of capacity limits.
