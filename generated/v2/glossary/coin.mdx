# Coin

A coin, in the cryptocurrency space, refers to the native digital asset of a specific blockchain network. Unlike tokens, which exist on top of an existing blockchain, a coin is used as the primary mechanism for paying transaction fees (gas), securing the network, and serving as a store of value or unit of account. Coins are fundamental to the protocol's existence; for example, the network cannot function without its native coin to incentivize validators and pay for computational resources within that specific ecosystem.

## Definition

A coin, in the cryptocurrency space, refers to the native digital asset of a specific blockchain network. Unlike tokens, which exist on top of an existing blockchain, a coin is used as the primary mechanism for paying transaction fees (gas), securing the network, and serving as a store of value or unit of account. Coins are fundamental to the protocol's existence; for example, the network cannot function without its native coin to incentivize validators and pay for computational resources within that specific ecosystem.

## Simple explanation

A coin is the native 'currency' of a specific blockchain's world. Think of it like using a specific type of token to play games at an arcade; that token only works in that arcade, and you need it to turn on the machines (the network).

## Why it matters

Coins provide the economic fuel for decentralized networks. They align the incentives of all participants, from users paying fees to validators securing the network, creating a self-sustaining economy that doesn't rely on traditional money.

## How it works

The coin is defined within the blockchain's core code. It is distributed via consensus rules (like mining rewards) and is held in public-private key pairs. Transactions involving the coin are validated and updated on the distributed ledger.

## Real-world example

Ether (ETH) is the native coin of the Ethereum network, used to pay for smart contract execution and network security.

## Advantages

- Necessary for native network operations
- Standardized unit of value within the blockchain
- Facilitates decentralized peer-to-peer exchange

## Limitations

- Volatility affects daily utility
- Limited use outside of the native ecosystem
- Often confused with broader token categories

## Common misconceptions

- Many people think all crypto assets are 'coins', but coins are native to their own blockchain, unlike tokens.
- Some believe coins are just digital money, ignoring their role as network fuel.
