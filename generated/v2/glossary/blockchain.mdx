# Blockchain

A blockchain is a distributed, immutable ledger technology that records transactions across a network of computers. Data is stored in 'blocks' that are linked chronologically using cryptographic hashes. Once data is verified and written, it is computationally impractical to alter or delete, ensuring a single, verifiable version of the truth without a central intermediary. This architecture provides transparency, security, and trust by requiring consensus among network participants rather than relying on a central authority.

## Definition

A blockchain is a distributed, immutable ledger technology that records transactions across a network of computers. Data is stored in 'blocks' that are linked chronologically using cryptographic hashes. Once data is verified and written, it is computationally impractical to alter or delete, ensuring a single, verifiable version of the truth without a central intermediary. This architecture provides transparency, security, and trust by requiring consensus among network participants rather than relying on a central authority.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a shared digital diary that everyone in a group can see but no one can erase or change. Whenever someone adds a new entry, everyone checks that it's legitimate. Because everyone has a copy, no one can cheat by changing the past.

## Why it matters

Blockchain enables peer-to-peer trust in digital environments without needing banks or corporations. It has the potential to revolutionize finance, supply chain management, and identity verification by reducing costs and increasing efficiency.

## How it works

Transactions are bundled into a block and broadcast to the network. Nodes verify the data against protocol rules. Once consensus is reached, the block is cryptographically linked to the previous block, creating a permanent, unchangeable chain.

## Real-world example

The Bitcoin blockchain functions as a global, decentralized payment network that operates without a central bank or government oversight.

## Advantages

- Provides high levels of data immutability
- Eliminates need for trusted third-party intermediaries
- Increases system-wide transparency and auditability

## Limitations

- Can be inefficient for high-frequency transactions
- Significant energy consumption in Proof of Work
- Difficult to modify data once recorded

## Common misconceptions

- Some believe blockchain is synonymous with Bitcoin, but it is just the underlying technology.
- Many mistakenly think blockchain is entirely anonymous, rather than pseudonymous.
