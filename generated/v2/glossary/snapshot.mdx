# Snapshot

Snapshot is a decentralized, gasless off-chain voting protocol widely utilized by DAOs to facilitate governance without requiring users to pay network transaction fees for every vote. By leveraging off-chain signatures, Snapshot allows token holders to sign messages confirming their vote, which are then verified and aggregated by the protocol. This mechanism separates the voting process from the main blockchain ledger, preventing network congestion and cost barriers while maintaining integrity through cryptographic proofs linked to the current on-chain token balances at a specific block height.

## Definition

Snapshot is a decentralized, gasless off-chain voting protocol widely utilized by DAOs to facilitate governance without requiring users to pay network transaction fees for every vote. By leveraging off-chain signatures, Snapshot allows token holders to sign messages confirming their vote, which are then verified and aggregated by the protocol. This mechanism separates the voting process from the main blockchain ledger, preventing network congestion and cost barriers while maintaining integrity through cryptographic proofs linked to the current on-chain token balances at a specific block height.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a class election where everyone has to pay a dollar to cast a vote. If the election takes all day, it becomes too expensive for many students. Snapshot is like a system where you sign your ballot on a piece of paper instead of paying. Your signature proves it is really you, and the teacher tallies up the votes later to see who won. It makes voting free and easy while still ensuring that only people with the right 'voting tokens' can participate.

## Why it matters

Snapshot is essential for DAO governance because it removes the financial barrier to participation, ensuring broader community involvement in decision-making. By eliminating gas fees, it empowers smaller token holders to vote frequently without sacrificing their assets, leading to more democratic and active protocol management.

## How it works

The protocol takes a 'snapshot' of a blockchain at a specific block height to record the exact token balances of all addresses. Users cast votes off-chain by signing a message with their private key, which is then verified by the Snapshot platform against those recorded balances. Once the voting period concludes, the results are calculated based on the weight of the tokens held at that initial snapshot time.

## Real-world example

Major decentralized protocols like Uniswap, Aave, and ENS utilize Snapshot for their governance proposals, allowing community members to vote on protocol upgrades, treasury distributions, and policy changes without incurring high Ethereum gas costs.

## Advantages

- Zero gas fees for voting
- High accessibility for small holders
- Fast and efficient governance
- Secure off-chain cryptographic signatures

## Limitations

- Off-chain data is not automatically enforced
- Requires manual execution of results
- Potential for off-chain centralized data handling

## Common misconceptions

- Many people believe Snapshot votes are automatically recorded on the blockchain ledger, but they remain off-chain.
- It is often mistaken for a wallet feature, when it is actually a distinct governance protocol platform.
