# Distributed Ledger Technology

Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) refers to a decentralized digital system for recording the transaction of assets in which the transactions and their details are recorded in multiple places at the same time. Unlike traditional databases, DLTs do not rely on a central data store or administrative functionality. Instead, each node within the network processes and verifies every item, generating a record of each item and creating a consensus on its truth. This architecture provides a shared, immutable history of information across a network of participants.

## Definition

Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) refers to a decentralized digital system for recording the transaction of assets in which the transactions and their details are recorded in multiple places at the same time. Unlike traditional databases, DLTs do not rely on a central data store or administrative functionality. Instead, each node within the network processes and verifies every item, generating a record of each item and creating a consensus on its truth. This architecture provides a shared, immutable history of information across a network of participants.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a class where everyone has their own notebook to record who owes who money for lunch. Instead of one teacher keeping the only book, every student writes down every transaction. If one student makes a mistake or tries to cheat, everyone else can compare their notebooks to see the truth. Because everyone has a copy of the records, the class doesn't need one person in charge to manage the money. It keeps everyone honest without needing a boss.

## Why it matters

DLT removes the need for central authorities like banks or clearinghouses, drastically reducing middleman costs and failure points. It fosters trust in environments where participants do not inherently trust one another by ensuring data integrity and transparency.

## How it works

The network consists of geographically dispersed nodes that maintain identical copies of the ledger. When a transaction occurs, it is broadcast to all nodes, which validate the data using pre-defined consensus algorithms. Once validated, the transaction is bundled into a block and permanently committed to the chain. This synchronization ensures that all participants possess a mathematically consistent view of the state of the system.

## Real-world example

The Hedera Hashgraph network uses DLT to provide fast, secure, and fair transaction logging for enterprise applications without traditional proof-of-work mining.

## Advantages

- Eliminates single points of failure
- Increases transparency and auditability
- Reduces dependency on intermediaries
- Enhances data security and resistance to tampering

## Limitations

- Higher latency compared to centralized databases
- Complex regulatory and legal compliance challenges
- Significant energy consumption for some consensus types

## Common misconceptions

- Many people believe DLT and blockchain are identical terms, but blockchain is actually a specific type of DLT.
- Some assume DLTs are always anonymous, but many enterprise DLTs are permissioned and require identity verification.
