# AML

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) refers to a comprehensive set of legal and procedural controls designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income. In the financial sector, AML mandates that institutions monitor customer transactions, verify identities, and report suspicious activities to regulatory authorities. Within the blockchain industry, AML compliance is increasingly critical for exchanges and protocols that interface with the traditional financial system, ensuring that digital assets are not utilized to facilitate illicit activities like terrorism financing or tax evasion.

## Definition

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) refers to a comprehensive set of legal and procedural controls designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income. In the financial sector, AML mandates that institutions monitor customer transactions, verify identities, and report suspicious activities to regulatory authorities. Within the blockchain industry, AML compliance is increasingly critical for exchanges and protocols that interface with the traditional financial system, ensuring that digital assets are not utilized to facilitate illicit activities like terrorism financing or tax evasion.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a criminal steals a bag of money and tries to hide it by buying a sandwich shop and claiming the stolen cash came from sandwich sales. AML is like the detective work banks do to catch this. They require you to show your ID and explain where your money comes from to make sure you aren't just 'cleaning' dirty money to make it look like you earned it honestly.

## Why it matters

AML protocols bridge the gap between anonymous blockchain transactions and the regulated financial world, which is essential for institutional adoption. Without strict AML compliance, blockchain networks risk being blacklisted by global regulators and losing access to traditional banking rails.

## How it works

Institutions employ Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures to verify user identities and analyze transaction patterns to identify anomalies or high-risk activity. If a suspicious pattern is detected, the institution files a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with government financial intelligence units. Blockchain analytics firms further this by tracking on-chain wallets for links to known illicit sources.

## Real-world example

Major centralized exchanges like Coinbase and Kraken utilize robust AML and KYC verification software to comply with international regulations and prevent platform abuse.

## Advantages

- Reduces criminal abuse of systems
- Increases institutional trust
- Enhances global financial security

## Limitations

- Increases friction for users
- Requires sensitive data collection
- High compliance implementation costs

## Common misconceptions

- Many assume AML is only for banking, but it increasingly applies to all crypto service providers. People often conflate AML with privacy loss, but it is specifically intended to track illegal activity rather than monitor legitimate personal spending.
