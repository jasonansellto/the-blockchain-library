# Token Burn

Token burning is a mechanism by which a portion of the total supply of a cryptocurrency is permanently removed from circulation. This is typically achieved by sending the tokens to a cryptographically verified 'eater address' or 'null address'—a wallet address for which the private key is inaccessible or does not exist. Because the private key is unknown, these tokens can never be spent, transferred, or recovered, effectively reducing the circulating supply and deflationary pressure on the tokenomics of the underlying blockchain protocol.

## Definition

Token burning is a mechanism by which a portion of the total supply of a cryptocurrency is permanently removed from circulation. This is typically achieved by sending the tokens to a cryptographically verified 'eater address' or 'null address'—a wallet address for which the private key is inaccessible or does not exist. Because the private key is unknown, these tokens can never be spent, transferred, or recovered, effectively reducing the circulating supply and deflationary pressure on the tokenomics of the underlying blockchain protocol.

## Simple explanation

Imagine you have a deck of cards and you decide to take five cards out of the game and lock them in a safe that can never be opened. Now, there are fewer cards in the game. Token burning is like that; crypto projects permanently destroy some of their coins so that fewer are available. By making the coins scarcer, they hope the remaining coins become more valuable, similar to how rare items become worth more when they are hard to find.

## Why it matters

Token burning serves as a powerful deflationary tool to manage supply-side economics in decentralized ecosystems. By reducing the total supply, projects can influence the long-term value of their assets and demonstrate commitment to token holders by limiting inflationary pressure.

## How it works

To burn tokens, a project initiates a transaction that sends the assets to a burn address, often represented as 0x000...000. Because no entity possesses the private key to this address, the tokens are rendered permanently inert and unusable on the blockchain. Once the transaction is confirmed on the ledger, the total supply is reduced, and the burn is publicly verifiable through block explorers by anyone monitoring the supply data.

## Real-world example

Binance performs quarterly 'BNB burns' to reduce the total supply of BNB to 100 million tokens, while Ethereum utilizes EIP-1559, which burns a portion of the transaction fees paid by users to maintain a balanced supply.

## Advantages

- Deflationary pressure on token supply
- Potentially increases token scarcity
- Demonstrates protocol sustainability to investors
- Reduces inflationary issuance rates

## Limitations

- Does not guarantee price appreciation
- Can be used to mask poor utility
- Irreversible process for the burned assets
- Requires constant supply management by developers

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe that burning tokens will automatically cause the price of the asset to increase significantly.
- People often think that burned tokens are stored in a hidden account that developers can access later.
