# Non-Fungible Token

A Non-Fungible Token (NFT) is a cryptographic asset on a blockchain with unique identification codes and metadata that distinguish it from all other tokens. Unlike fungible assets like Bitcoin or fiat currency, which are interchangeable and identical in value, an NFT is unique and cannot be replaced with an identical item. This property of non-fungibility makes NFTs ideal for representing ownership of unique digital items, such as art, music, gaming assets, or real-world property deeds, within a verifiable, decentralized system.

## Definition

A Non-Fungible Token (NFT) is a cryptographic asset on a blockchain with unique identification codes and metadata that distinguish it from all other tokens. Unlike fungible assets like Bitcoin or fiat currency, which are interchangeable and identical in value, an NFT is unique and cannot be replaced with an identical item. This property of non-fungibility makes NFTs ideal for representing ownership of unique digital items, such as art, music, gaming assets, or real-world property deeds, within a verifiable, decentralized system.

## Simple explanation

Think of 'fungible' as money; a dollar bill in your pocket is the same as any other dollar. 'Non-fungible' means unique. A signed first-edition book or a specific piece of original art is non-fungible because there is nothing else exactly like it. An NFT is the digital version of that 'one-of-a-kind' item.

## Why it matters

NFTs allow for the first time in history a method to prove scarcity and ownership of digital goods. This creates the foundation for a true digital economy where assets can be owned, not just licensed.

## How it works

NFTs are created using token standards (like ERC-721 or ERC-1155) on a blockchain. These standards define the rules for creating unique tokens that contain specific metadata. Once deployed, the blockchain acts as a public ledger that records exactly who owns which token at any given moment, making it impossible to duplicate or forge ownership.

## Real-world example

The 'Everydays: The First 5000 Days' artwork by Beeple is a iconic example of an NFT that proved the potential for high-value digital art sales.

## Advantages

- Unique and non-duplicatable ownership
- Verifiable digital scarcity
- Easily transferable global assets

## Limitations

- High energy consumption (depending on the chain)
- Market is often highly speculative
- Lack of legal clarity in some jurisdictions

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe an NFT is synonymous with 'digital art,' ignoring its use in gaming, identity, and real estate.
- People often think owning an NFT means they own the underlying copyright of the artwork.
