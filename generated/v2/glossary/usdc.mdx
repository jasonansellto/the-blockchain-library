# USDC

USDC (USD Coin) is a fiat-backed stablecoin that is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar. Managed by the Centre Consortium, which includes Circle and Coinbase, USDC is designed for transparency and regulatory compliance. It is fully backed by cash and short-dated US Treasury bonds held in regulated financial institutions. Its commitment to regular third-party audits and adherence to US financial regulations has made it a preferred stablecoin for institutional investors and regulated DeFi applications.

## Definition

USDC (USD Coin) is a fiat-backed stablecoin that is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar. Managed by the Centre Consortium, which includes Circle and Coinbase, USDC is designed for transparency and regulatory compliance. It is fully backed by cash and short-dated US Treasury bonds held in regulated financial institutions. Its commitment to regular third-party audits and adherence to US financial regulations has made it a preferred stablecoin for institutional investors and regulated DeFi applications.

## Simple explanation

USDC is like a regulated, high-quality digital dollar. It is run by big companies that must follow strict rules, and they keep real money in banks to ensure your digital dollars are always safe and ready to trade for real ones.

## Why it matters

USDC provides the necessary trust and compliance layer for large-scale corporate adoption of blockchain technology. It bridges traditional high-finance compliance with the speed and efficiency of decentralized networks.

## How it works

Users send fiat currency to the issuing entity, which then mints an equivalent amount of USDC tokens on the blockchain. The fiat is deposited into audited bank accounts. To exit, the user sends tokens to the issuer, who burns them and transfers the corresponding fiat back to the user's bank account.

## Real-world example

Widely used on Ethereum, Solana, and Base networks for trading.

## Advantages

- Strong regulatory compliance
- Highly liquid and trustworthy
- Transparent financial audits

## Limitations

- Centralized control allows account freezing
- Requires adherence to KYC/AML
- Dependent on regulated banking partners

## Common misconceptions

- People often think it is fully decentralized. Others mistakenly believe it earns interest automatically.
