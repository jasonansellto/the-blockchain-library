# Governance Proposal

A governance proposal is a formal request for action submitted by a community member to the DAO or protocol participants. It outlines specific changes, such as protocol parameter adjustments, treasury expenditures, or code modifications. Proposals undergo a formal review process where participants evaluate the technical implications, economic impact, and strategic alignment of the request. Once submitted, the proposal serves as the foundation for the voting process, ensuring that the community has a clear, documented mandate before any permanent changes are written into the protocol.

## Definition

A governance proposal is a formal request for action submitted by a community member to the DAO or protocol participants. It outlines specific changes, such as protocol parameter adjustments, treasury expenditures, or code modifications. Proposals undergo a formal review process where participants evaluate the technical implications, economic impact, and strategic alignment of the request. Once submitted, the proposal serves as the foundation for the voting process, ensuring that the community has a clear, documented mandate before any permanent changes are written into the protocol.

## Simple explanation

A proposal is like a formal suggestion or a petition presented to a group. If you want to change the rules of your digital club, you write up a proposal explaining what you want to do and why. Everyone reads it, asks questions, and then gets to vote on it.

## Why it matters

Proposals are the primary vehicle for introducing change in a decentralized network. Without this structure, a DAO would have no formal way to gather community consensus on important decisions.

## How it works

A user drafts a proposal detailing the requested changes and submits it to the protocol interface. This initiates a discussion phase, followed by a formal voting window. If the proposal reaches the required support, the changes are adopted by the system.

## Real-world example

A Compound Finance Improvement Proposal (CIP) suggests changing interest rate models for specific assets.

## Advantages

- Structured decision pathway
- Creates public documentation
- Enables diverse community input
- Clear record of intent

## Limitations

- Spam proposals
- Complex technical documentation requirements
- Low community participation rates

## Common misconceptions

- Proposals are not just ideas; they are technical specifications that must be vetted. Some mistakenly believe a proposal is binding before it has been voted on.
