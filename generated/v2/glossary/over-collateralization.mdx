# Over-collateralization

Over-collateralization is a decentralized finance mechanism requiring a borrower to pledge assets with a market value greater than the value of the loan being requested. This buffer serves as a critical safety net against price volatility in the underlying crypto assets. By mandating a collateral-to-debt ratio consistently above 100%, decentralized protocols mitigate counterparty risk without the need for traditional credit scores, ensuring that lenders are shielded from potential defaults during adverse market downturns where the collateral value might plummet rapidly.

## Definition

Over-collateralization is a decentralized finance mechanism requiring a borrower to pledge assets with a market value greater than the value of the loan being requested. This buffer serves as a critical safety net against price volatility in the underlying crypto assets. By mandating a collateral-to-debt ratio consistently above 100%, decentralized protocols mitigate counterparty risk without the need for traditional credit scores, ensuring that lenders are shielded from potential defaults during adverse market downturns where the collateral value might plummet rapidly.

## Simple explanation

Imagine you want to borrow $100 from a friend, but to prove you will pay it back, you have to hand them $150 worth of gold coins as a security deposit first. If you don't pay the money back, your friend keeps the gold to cover their losses. In the crypto world, because digital coins change price very quickly, people have to lock up much more money than they are borrowing to make sure the lender never loses out, even if the price of the coins suddenly drops.

## Why it matters

Over-collateralization is the cornerstone of trustless lending in DeFi, allowing participants to access liquidity without requiring a centralized intermediary or identity verification. It provides the financial security necessary to sustain decentralized markets, preventing insolvency during extreme market volatility.

## How it works

A user interacts with a smart contract, depositing assets such as ETH or BTC into a secure vault. The protocol assigns a specific collateral ratio, typically requiring the deposit to be worth 150% or more of the debt issued. The smart contract continuously monitors the value of the locked assets against the borrowed debt, and if the value falls below the defined threshold, the contract automatically triggers a liquidation process to recover the debt.

## Real-world example

MakerDAO is the most prominent example, where users deposit ETH into a Vault to mint the DAI stablecoin. To maintain the peg, users must ensure their collateral ratio stays well above the required minimum, otherwise, the protocol sells their collateral to cover the debt.

## Advantages

- Eliminates reliance on centralized credit agencies
- Reduces default risk for lenders
- Enables permissionless lending for global users
- Ensures protocol solvency during market crashes

## Limitations

- Inefficient use of capital for the borrower
- High risk of liquidation during market volatility
- Requires significant upfront capital investment

## Common misconceptions

- People often think over-collateralization is only for high-risk assets when it is actually a requirement for stablecoin stability. It is a mistake to believe this process is inefficient, as it provides security that traditional banking requires via physical assets.
- Some believe that lenders lose money when a loan is liquidated, but in reality, the protocol ensures the debt is covered before losses reach the lender.
