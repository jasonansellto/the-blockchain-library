# RWA

RWA, or Real World Assets, refers to physical or financial assets that exist outside of the blockchain but are represented on-chain via tokenization. This category encompasses a vast spectrum, including real estate, precious metals, treasury bills, corporate bonds, and carbon credits. By bringing these assets on-chain, investors can trade them in the decentralized finance (DeFi) ecosystem, effectively bridging traditional finance (TradFi) with the speed, transparency, and global reach of decentralized networks.

## Definition

RWA, or Real World Assets, refers to physical or financial assets that exist outside of the blockchain but are represented on-chain via tokenization. This category encompasses a vast spectrum, including real estate, precious metals, treasury bills, corporate bonds, and carbon credits. By bringing these assets on-chain, investors can trade them in the decentralized finance (DeFi) ecosystem, effectively bridging traditional finance (TradFi) with the speed, transparency, and global reach of decentralized networks.

## Simple explanation

RWA stands for 'Real World Assets.' It is the practice of taking things from the physical world that have value—like gold, houses, or government debt—and creating a digital version of them that you can use on a blockchain.

## Why it matters

RWAs bring stable, high-value collateral into DeFi, which is crucial for the maturation of the ecosystem. It allows for the creation of sustainable yields that aren't solely dependent on crypto-native volatility.

## How it works

The process requires an off-chain valuation and legal verification of the asset's existence. An oracle or custodian then confirms this status to the blockchain. Once verified, a token is issued that represents the ownership or the right to the cash flow generated by that real-world asset.

## Real-world example

MakerDAO utilizes RWA by incorporating real-world loans and treasury bonds as collateral to back the issuance of their DAI stablecoin.

## Advantages

- Integration of sustainable real-world yields into DeFi
- Increased collateral variety for lending protocols
- Better link between crypto and traditional economy
- Lower risk compared to crypto-native assets

## Limitations

- Reliability of oracles and data feeds
- Legal enforcement across different jurisdictions
- Centralization risks in asset custody

## Common misconceptions

- Some believe RWAs will eliminate banks entirely, but they usually require banking partnerships to function. Many also think all RWAs are decentralized, when most rely on centralized entities for legal backing.
