# Tokenized Bonds

Tokenized bonds represent debt obligations that are represented as digital tokens on a blockchain. Instead of holding traditional paper certificates, investors hold tokens that entitle them to regular interest (coupon) payments and the repayment of the principal amount at maturity. This process streamlines the bond issuance lifecycle, reducing the role of intermediaries, and enabling real-time settlement and transparency for both institutional and individual bondholders.

## Definition

Tokenized bonds represent debt obligations that are represented as digital tokens on a blockchain. Instead of holding traditional paper certificates, investors hold tokens that entitle them to regular interest (coupon) payments and the repayment of the principal amount at maturity. This process streamlines the bond issuance lifecycle, reducing the role of intermediaries, and enabling real-time settlement and transparency for both institutional and individual bondholders.

## Simple explanation

A bond is essentially a loan you make to a government or company. A tokenized bond is just a digital version of that loan contract. Instead of complicated paperwork, you hold a digital token that automatically pays you your interest payments.

## Why it matters

Tokenization can significantly reduce the 'middleman' fees in the massive bond market, which is currently inefficient. It also opens up bond investing to retail investors who were previously ignored.

## How it works

An issuer creates a smart contract that dictates the bond's interest rate, payout schedule, and maturity date. Investors purchase the tokens, and the smart contract automatically handles the interest distributions to token holders. When the bond matures, the smart contract triggers the return of the principal.

## Real-world example

The European Investment Bank (EIB) has issued digital bonds on the Ethereum blockchain, showing institutional trust in the technology.

## Advantages

- Reduced transaction costs and fees
- Faster issuance and settlement times
- Increased transparency of interest payments
- Access to global retail investors

## Limitations

- Integration with legacy banking systems
- Potential regulatory complexity for cross-border issuances
- Technical debt and security vulnerabilities

## Common misconceptions

- Many people assume tokenized bonds are identical to crypto bonds, but they are generally regulated instruments. Some believe they are always tradable 24/7, though issuers often place restrictions.
