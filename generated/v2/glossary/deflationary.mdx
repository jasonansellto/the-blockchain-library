# Deflationary

A deflationary economic model in the context of blockchain refers to a tokenomic structure where the total circulating supply of an asset decreases over time. This is typically achieved through mechanisms like token burning, where tokens are permanently sent to unspendable addresses, or fee-burning protocols. By reducing the supply relative to demand, a deflationary model aims to create scarcity, potentially impacting the asset's value. In Ethereum's case, EIP-1559 introduced a mechanism that burns a portion of transaction fees, potentially making ETH deflationary depending on network activity.

## Definition

A deflationary economic model in the context of blockchain refers to a tokenomic structure where the total circulating supply of an asset decreases over time. This is typically achieved through mechanisms like token burning, where tokens are permanently sent to unspendable addresses, or fee-burning protocols. By reducing the supply relative to demand, a deflationary model aims to create scarcity, potentially impacting the asset's value. In Ethereum's case, EIP-1559 introduced a mechanism that burns a portion of transaction fees, potentially making ETH deflationary depending on network activity.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a rare trading card game where every time someone plays a card, that card is destroyed. Because fewer and fewer cards are left in existence, the remaining ones become much harder to find and arguably more valuable. In crypto, a deflationary token is like those cards; when certain network actions happen, tokens are deleted forever, shrinking the total supply available for everyone else.

## Why it matters

Deflationary mechanisms provide a programmatic approach to managing supply-side pressure on an asset's price. This affects investor sentiment and the long-term utility of the blockchain by aligning the growth of network usage with the scarcity of its native asset.

## How it works

The mechanism relies on smart contract functions that systematically remove tokens from circulation. For example, Ethereum's EIP-1559 burns the base fee of every transaction, effectively removing that ETH from the ecosystem permanently. When the amount of tokens burned exceeds the amount newly minted as rewards, the net supply of the asset decreases, creating a deflationary cycle.

## Real-world example

The Ethereum network is the most prominent example, specifically following the implementation of EIP-1559 and the transition to Proof-of-Stake, which significantly reduced the issuance of new ETH.

## Advantages

- Potential for increased long-term scarcity
- Incentivizes holding assets over time
- Supports network value during high activity

## Limitations

- Could discourage active spending or usage
- Price is still influenced by market demand
- Highly dependent on specific network conditions

## Common misconceptions

- People often think that all deflationary tokens are guaranteed to increase in price regardless of demand. Another common misconception is that deflationary tokens are always better than inflationary ones for economic growth.
