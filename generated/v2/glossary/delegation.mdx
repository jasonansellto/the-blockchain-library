# Delegation

Delegation is a mechanism within delegated proof-of-stake (DPoS) and similar blockchain consensus models whereby token holders assign their voting power or staking weight to a chosen validator or delegate. By delegating their assets, users participate in the network's security and governance processes without needing to run their own infrastructure. The delegate acts on behalf of the delegators to propose blocks, validate transactions, and cast governance votes, often distributing a portion of the earned rewards back to the delegators proportionally to their stake.

## Definition

Delegation is a mechanism within delegated proof-of-stake (DPoS) and similar blockchain consensus models whereby token holders assign their voting power or staking weight to a chosen validator or delegate. By delegating their assets, users participate in the network's security and governance processes without needing to run their own infrastructure. The delegate acts on behalf of the delegators to propose blocks, validate transactions, and cast governance votes, often distributing a portion of the earned rewards back to the delegators proportionally to their stake.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a big company where every shareholder gets a vote, but keeping track of thousands of votes is too slow. Instead, you give your voting power to someone you trust—a 'delegate'—to vote for you. In blockchain, you give your coins to a professional validator who handles the heavy lifting of running the network. In exchange, you get a small share of the rewards they earn, and they get to use your weight to keep the network secure.

## Why it matters

Delegation makes blockchain networks more inclusive by allowing small token holders to contribute to network security and earn yield. It balances the need for high-performance consensus with the democratic ideals of decentralized governance, ensuring that network operation remains accessible to non-technical users.

## How it works

Users interact with a protocol's staking contract to 'delegate' their tokens to a specific validator node. The blockchain protocol records this stake, which increases the validator's weight in the consensus selection process. The protocol periodically calculates rewards based on performance and distributes them among the validator and its delegators, minus any commission fees set by the operator.

## Real-world example

In the Cosmos (ATOM) network, users delegate their tokens to validators via wallets like Keplr to secure the network and earn staking rewards.

## Advantages

- Enables passive income for token holders
- Lowers barrier to network participation
- Increases network security via distributed stake
- No need to manage complex server infrastructure

## Limitations

- Risk of slashing due to validator misconduct
- Potential for centralization around large validators
- Lock-up periods limit liquidity of staked assets
- Dependency on validator operational performance

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe delegating tokens means giving up ownership of their assets to the validator. You always retain custody of your tokens and can undelegate them at any time.
- People often assume delegating is the same as mining. While both secure the network, delegation does not require specialized hardware or high energy consumption.
