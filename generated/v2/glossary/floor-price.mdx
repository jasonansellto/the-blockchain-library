# Floor Price

The floor price is the lowest cost at which an NFT from a specific collection can be purchased on the secondary market. It serves as a primary benchmark for the market valuation of a collection, indicating the entry price for new participants. As supply and demand fluctuate, the floor price changes in real-time, often serving as a signal for the perceived sentiment, liquidity, and overall health of an NFT project among traders and investors.

## Definition

The floor price is the lowest cost at which an NFT from a specific collection can be purchased on the secondary market. It serves as a primary benchmark for the market valuation of a collection, indicating the entry price for new participants. As supply and demand fluctuate, the floor price changes in real-time, often serving as a signal for the perceived sentiment, liquidity, and overall health of an NFT project among traders and investors.

## Simple explanation

The floor price is the 'cheapest ticket' to get into a collection. If you want to buy an NFT from a popular set, the floor price is the lowest amount of cryptocurrency you need to spend right now to own one of the items in that group.

## Why it matters

The floor price acts as a barometer for market interest and project success. Investors frequently monitor this metric to assess whether to buy, sell, or hold their assets.

## How it works

NFT marketplaces aggregate all listed items for a specific smart contract. The algorithm automatically identifies the lowest-priced 'Buy Now' listing and displays it as the floor price. This value changes instantly whenever a user buys the cheapest item or lists a new item for a lower price.

## Real-world example

If a user goes to OpenSea to view a popular project and sees the cheapest item listed at 2.5 ETH, that 2.5 ETH figure is the current floor price.

## Advantages

- Provides immediate liquidity signal
- Helps gauge market sentiment
- Simplifies entry-level price discovery

## Limitations

- Can be manipulated by 'wash trading'
- Does not reflect value of 'rare' items
- Highly volatile during market downturns

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe the floor price represents the true value of every item in the collection.
- Some assume a high floor price guarantees a profitable investment.
