# Quorum

A quorum is the minimum number of votes or token-represented weight required for a governance proposal to be considered valid and actionable. It serves as a critical security threshold to prevent minority rule or malicious takeovers by small, centralized groups. If a proposal does not reach the quorum, it fails regardless of whether the majority of voters supported it. Establishing an appropriate quorum is a balancing act; it must be high enough to ensure sufficient community participation but low enough to prevent stagnation.

## Definition

A quorum is the minimum number of votes or token-represented weight required for a governance proposal to be considered valid and actionable. It serves as a critical security threshold to prevent minority rule or malicious takeovers by small, centralized groups. If a proposal does not reach the quorum, it fails regardless of whether the majority of voters supported it. Establishing an appropriate quorum is a balancing act; it must be high enough to ensure sufficient community participation but low enough to prevent stagnation.

## Simple explanation

Quorum is the 'minimum attendance' rule. Just like a real meeting might need at least half the board members present to make a decision, a DAO needs a certain amount of tokens to participate before a vote can count. It keeps things fair.

## Why it matters

Quorum prevents a small group of attackers from passing malicious proposals by voting when nobody else is paying attention. It ensures that decisions reflect the genuine will of the broader community.

## How it works

The voting contract monitors the total number of votes cast. If the tally exceeds the pre-defined percentage of total supply, the quorum is satisfied and the vote results are valid.

## Real-world example

Yearn Finance requires a specific quorum threshold of total YFI tokens before governance changes can be finalized.

## Advantages

- Prevents minority rule
- Increases decision legitimacy
- Protects against low-participation attacks

## Limitations

- Can cause voting stagnation
- Difficult to hit in large DAOs
- High threshold discourages small voters

## Common misconceptions

- People often think a higher quorum is always safer, but it can actually kill a protocol's ability to react to emergencies. Many confuse quorum with simple majority.
