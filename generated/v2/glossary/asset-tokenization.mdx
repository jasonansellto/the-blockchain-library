# Asset Tokenization

The process of representing ownership, claims, or economic rights in an asset using blockchain-based tokens.

## Definition

The process of representing ownership, claims, or economic rights in an asset using blockchain-based tokens.

## Why it matters

Tokenization concepts connect blockchain infrastructure with legal or economic rights in real-world assets.

## How it works

The process begins by auditing and valuing the asset, which is then held in a legal structure like a Special Purpose Vehicle. Smart contracts are developed to define token utility and ownership rights. Finally, tokens are minted on a blockchain, verified by auditors, and distributed to investors through regulated platforms.

## Real-world example

Franklin Templeton uses the Stellar blockchain to tokenize its OnChain U.S. Government Money Fund, allowing shareholders to track their holdings on a public ledger.

## Advantages

- Increased market liquidity for illiquid assets
- Lower transaction costs through automation
- Global 24/7 market access
- Greater transparency and auditability

## Limitations

- Complex legal and regulatory compliance requirements
- Interoperability issues between different blockchains
- Dependency on off-chain asset custodians

## Common misconceptions

- People often think tokenization is just for digital-native assets, but it actually bridges physical assets to the blockchain. Another misconception is that tokens are always decentralized, but most RWA tokens are highly centralized by design for compliance.
