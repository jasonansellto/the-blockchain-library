# Tokenized Real Estate

Tokenized real estate is the process of putting property ownership on a blockchain by dividing the value of a building or land into digital tokens. This structure allows investors to own a 'fraction' of a property, gain exposure to price appreciation, and receive rental income without needing the massive capital required to buy an entire property. Each token represents legal fractional ownership, recorded on the blockchain, and is subject to the specific property's legal and zoning regulations.

## Definition

Tokenized real estate is the process of putting property ownership on a blockchain by dividing the value of a building or land into digital tokens. This structure allows investors to own a 'fraction' of a property, gain exposure to price appreciation, and receive rental income without needing the massive capital required to buy an entire property. Each token represents legal fractional ownership, recorded on the blockchain, and is subject to the specific property's legal and zoning regulations.

## Simple explanation

Imagine you can buy 1% of a commercial building for $500. Instead of needing a mortgage and a massive down payment, you buy tokens that represent your small share. If the building collects rent, you get your fair cut sent to your digital wallet.

## Why it matters

It turns one of the world's least liquid asset classes—real estate—into something that can be traded much more easily. It lowers the barrier to entry for property wealth.

## How it works

A legal entity acquires the property, and the ownership is split into digital tokens representing fractional interests. Smart contracts manage the revenue distribution from rental income. Investors purchase these tokens via a regulated platform, and ownership status is updated in real-time.

## Real-world example

Blocksquare provides the infrastructure for real estate tokenization, allowing marketplaces to offer properties in fractionalized digital shares.

## Advantages

- Lower capital requirements for property investing
- Increased liquidity for commercial real estate
- Automated, efficient rental income distribution
- Easier portfolio diversification across regions

## Limitations

- Legal and jurisdictional complexities of property law
- Maintenance of the underlying physical building
- Risk of illiquid secondary markets

## Common misconceptions

- A common misconception is that owning the token automatically makes you the landlord with rights to manage the property. People also think you can sell these tokens as easily as Bitcoin, but they often have transfer restrictions.
