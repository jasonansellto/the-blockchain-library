# Decentralized Exchange

A Decentralized Exchange (DEX) is a peer-to-peer marketplace where cryptocurrency transactions occur directly between users without an intermediary. Unlike centralized exchanges, which hold user funds in custodial wallets and manage order books, DEXs utilize smart contracts to automate trades. They frequently leverage Automated Market Makers (AMMs) or liquidity pools to enable continuous trading. By removing the need for a trusted third party, DEXs facilitate non-custodial trading, where users retain full control of their private keys and assets throughout the trade execution process.

## Definition

A Decentralized Exchange (DEX) is a peer-to-peer marketplace where cryptocurrency transactions occur directly between users without an intermediary. Unlike centralized exchanges, which hold user funds in custodial wallets and manage order books, DEXs utilize smart contracts to automate trades. They frequently leverage Automated Market Makers (AMMs) or liquidity pools to enable continuous trading. By removing the need for a trusted third party, DEXs facilitate non-custodial trading, where users retain full control of their private keys and assets throughout the trade execution process.

## Simple explanation

A centralized exchange is like a bank where you deposit your money for them to hold. A decentralized exchange is like a digital street market where you trade directly with a robot. You never give your money to a company; you swap tokens directly from your own digital wallet, keeping full control of your keys the entire time.

## Why it matters

DEXs are fundamental to Web3 because they eliminate the risk of exchange bankruptcy, theft of customer funds, and arbitrary trading restrictions. They provide global access to liquid markets without requiring identity verification or corporate approval.

## How it works

DEXs operate via smart contracts deployed on a blockchain. Users deposit assets into liquidity pools to provide market depth, and when a trader wants to swap tokens, the smart contract calculates the price based on pool ratios and executes the swap instantly. The transaction is then recorded directly on-chain, ensuring settlement happens without any middleman.

## Real-world example

Uniswap is the largest decentralized exchange protocol, allowing users to trade tokens on Ethereum without an order book by using a constant product market maker model.

## Advantages

- Non-custodial asset management
- No KYC requirements
- 24/7 global market access

## Limitations

- Potential for smart contract bugs
- High network gas fees
- Risk of impermanent loss

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe DEXs are safer than centralized ones, yet smart contract risk is a significant new threat.
- People assume DEXs are always cheaper, failing to account for blockchain transaction fees.
