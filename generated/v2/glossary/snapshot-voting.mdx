# Snapshot Voting

Snapshot voting is an off-chain, gasless voting mechanism used by many DAOs to gauge community sentiment. Because it operates off-chain, voting does not require transaction fees, allowing for more frequent and inclusive participation. Instead of executing code on the main blockchain, Snapshot records the 'snapshot' of token balances at a specific block height. While the results are off-chain, they are often used to signal intent before a final on-chain vote or to manage non-critical decisions like social community guidelines.

## Definition

## Simple explanation

Snapshot voting is like an online survey that counts how many tokens you have without charging you fees. You don't have to pay money to send a 'vote' transaction. It makes it very cheap and fast for everyone to share their opinion on how the project should run.

## Why it matters

It lowers the barrier to entry, allowing members with fewer assets to participate in governance without being priced out by transaction fees. It is essential for high-frequency or informal community decision-making.

## How it works

The platform 'takes a snapshot' of user wallet balances. Users sign a message with their private key to cast a vote. These signatures are stored off-chain, then tallied to reflect the weight of the tokens held by each voter.

## Real-world example

The Snapshot platform is the industry standard used by thousands of DAOs, including Aave and Uniswap, for community signaling.

## Advantages

- No gas fees
- Easy for participants
- Fast voting speed
- Highly accessible

## Limitations

- Off-chain results are not binding
- Trust in the platform interface
- Vulnerability to Sybil attacks

## Common misconceptions

- Many think Snapshot votes are legally binding on-chain, but they are often just a signaling tool. People assume the lack of gas fees makes it 'less secure' than on-chain voting.
