# MakerDAO

MakerDAO is a decentralized autonomous organization that operates the Maker Protocol on the Ethereum blockchain, facilitating the issuance of the decentralized stablecoin DAI. It functions as a two-token system where DAI is soft-pegged to the US Dollar, and MKR serves as the governance token. The protocol utilizes a system of smart contracts known as Vaults, which require users to over-collateralize digital assets to generate DAI. Governance of the protocol, including parameters like stability fees, is decentralized among MKR token holders.

## Definition

MakerDAO is a decentralized autonomous organization that operates the Maker Protocol on the Ethereum blockchain, facilitating the issuance of the decentralized stablecoin DAI. It functions as a two-token system where DAI is soft-pegged to the US Dollar, and MKR serves as the governance token. The protocol utilizes a system of smart contracts known as Vaults, which require users to over-collateralize digital assets to generate DAI. Governance of the protocol, including parameters like stability fees, is decentralized among MKR token holders.

## Simple explanation

Think of MakerDAO like a decentralized digital bank that doesn't need a human manager. If you want to borrow money, you give them a valuable item (like gold, but in crypto) that is worth more than the loan. They give you DAI, a digital dollar, in exchange. If you pay back the loan, you get your gold back. The community of people who hold a special token called MKR votes on the rules, like how much extra you have to pay to borrow, making sure the system stays safe and stable.

## Why it matters

MakerDAO is a foundational pillar of the DeFi ecosystem because it provides a reliable, censorship-resistant stablecoin that powers countless other applications. It demonstrates how decentralized governance can successfully manage complex financial systems without reliance on traditional banking intermediaries.

## How it works

Users deposit supported crypto assets into Maker Vaults, which locks the assets as collateral. Once locked, the protocol mints DAI stablecoins based on the collateral value, maintaining a specific collateralization ratio. If the collateral value drops significantly, the protocol automatically triggers a liquidation process to cover the debt. MKR holders vote on global variables such as debt ceilings and interest rates to manage systemic risk.

## Real-world example

Users can deposit ETH into the Maker Protocol via the Oasis dashboard to mint DAI, which can then be used across various DeFi platforms for lending or trading.

## Advantages

- Decentralized and censorship-resistant stablecoin issuance
- Transparent, community-governed financial protocol
- Enables leverage without traditional banking intermediaries
- Highly audited and battle-tested smart contract infrastructure

## Limitations

- Risk of liquidation during market volatility
- Governance centralization via large token holders
- Dependency on external price oracles for valuation
- Complex user experience for managing collateral ratios

## Common misconceptions

- It is often assumed that DAI is backed purely by cash reserves like centralized stablecoins, rather than being backed by digital assets.
- People think MakerDAO is a single company, but it is actually a decentralized organization governed by a global community of token holders.
