# Fiat Currency

Fiat currency is a legal tender whose value is not derived from a physical commodity like gold or silver, but is instead established by government decree. It functions as a medium of exchange because of public trust in the issuing government and the underlying economic stability of the state. Central banks manage the supply of fiat currency through monetary policy, including interest rates and open market operations, to regulate inflation and stimulate economic growth, though it is inherently susceptible to devaluation through excessive printing.

## Definition

Fiat currency is a legal tender whose value is not derived from a physical commodity like gold or silver, but is instead established by government decree. It functions as a medium of exchange because of public trust in the issuing government and the underlying economic stability of the state. Central banks manage the supply of fiat currency through monetary policy, including interest rates and open market operations, to regulate inflation and stimulate economic growth, though it is inherently susceptible to devaluation through excessive printing.

## Simple explanation

Imagine if you could only trade at the school cafeteria using tokens issued by the principal. These tokens aren't made of gold or anything valuable; they only work because everyone agrees the principal says they are worth lunch. Fiat money is just like those tokens, but on a national scale. Your dollars, euros, and yen have value simply because the government says they do and everyone trusts that they can be used to pay taxes or buy things.

## Why it matters

Fiat currency serves as the primary bridge between the traditional banking system and the crypto ecosystem, forming the collateral for many stablecoins. Understanding fiat is essential to comprehending the valuation of digital assets and the regulatory pressures facing decentralized finance.

## How it works

Fiat is created and regulated by central banks through monetary policy and the fractional reserve banking system. Governments issue physical and digital cash, and manage its circulation to control inflation and liquidity. It derives its value primarily from the economic productivity and legal mandate of the sovereign state issuing it.

## Real-world example

The United States Dollar (USD) is the most widely used fiat currency globally and serves as the standard peg for major stablecoins like USDC and USDT.

## Advantages

- Universally accepted for daily transactions
- Regulated by central authorities
- Flexible monetary policy tools

## Limitations

- Susceptible to inflationary pressure
- Subject to government control
- Requires institutional trust

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe fiat is backed by gold, but it has not been tied to gold since the collapse of the Bretton Woods system. People often think the government creates all money, when most money is actually created by private commercial banks through credit issuance.
