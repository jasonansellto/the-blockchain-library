# Data Integrity

Data integrity in blockchain refers to the assurance that the information stored on the distributed ledger remains accurate, complete, and unaltered throughout its entire lifecycle. This is achieved through cryptographic primitives, such as hashing and digital signatures, and consensus mechanisms that prevent unauthorized modifications. By creating an immutable record where every block is cryptographically linked to the previous one, blockchain technology ensures that once data is recorded, it is practically impossible to change without detection.

## Definition

Data integrity in blockchain refers to the assurance that the information stored on the distributed ledger remains accurate, complete, and unaltered throughout its entire lifecycle. This is achieved through cryptographic primitives, such as hashing and digital signatures, and consensus mechanisms that prevent unauthorized modifications. By creating an immutable record where every block is cryptographically linked to the previous one, blockchain technology ensures that once data is recorded, it is practically impossible to change without detection.

## Simple explanation

Imagine writing in a notebook using a permanent ink that cannot be erased. If someone tried to scratch out a line or add a new one, the paper would tear and everyone would notice. Data integrity in blockchain is like having that permanent, transparent notebook where everyone can see that the records are original and haven't been tampered with.

## Why it matters

Data integrity is the foundational pillar of trust in decentralized systems. It ensures that financial transactions, identity records, and supply chain data remain reliable, preventing fraud and providing a single, indisputable 'source of truth' for all participants.

## How it works

Blockchain ensures integrity by hashing every block of data and including that hash in the next block. If any single character in a past block is altered, its unique hash changes, breaking the cryptographic chain and alerting the entire network. Consensus rules then automatically reject any ledger version that fails these cryptographic consistency checks.

## Real-world example

Supply chain platforms like IBM Food Trust use blockchain to ensure the integrity of food source data, preventing counterfeit items and ensuring public health safety.

## Advantages

- Provides immutable and tamper-evident records
- Establishes trust in decentralized environments
- Enables auditable and transparent history

## Limitations

- Garbage in, garbage out if initial data is false
- Requires secure key management to prevent unauthorized entry
- Data once written is difficult to delete or redact

## Common misconceptions

- Many people believe blockchain is completely immune to fraud, but it only protects the integrity of the data once it is on the ledger.
- Users often confuse data integrity with privacy, thinking that an immutable ledger also guarantees data will remain hidden from others.
