# Market Capitalization

Market capitalization, or market cap, in the context of cryptocurrencies and NFTs, is a metric used to determine the relative size and total value of a specific asset or collection. It is calculated by multiplying the current floor price of an asset by the total circulating supply of that asset class. While it serves as a common benchmark for investor sentiment and liquidity, it is important to note that in the NFT space, market cap can be highly volatile due to the illiquid nature of individual unique tokens.

## Definition

Market capitalization, or market cap, in the context of cryptocurrencies and NFTs, is a metric used to determine the relative size and total value of a specific asset or collection. It is calculated by multiplying the current floor price of an asset by the total circulating supply of that asset class. While it serves as a common benchmark for investor sentiment and liquidity, it is important to note that in the NFT space, market cap can be highly volatile due to the illiquid nature of individual unique tokens.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a collection of rare trading cards. If one card sells for $10 and there are 1,000 of them in total, the 'market cap' of that collection is $10,000. It is a quick way to see how much the entire group of items is worth according to the most recent price. It helps you compare the overall popularity and value of different projects at a glance.

## Why it matters

Market capitalization provides a standardized metric for comparing the scale of different digital asset projects across the blockchain ecosystem. It assists investors and collectors in assessing the overall health, market dominance, and relative valuation of a project compared to its competitors.

## How it works

The metric functions by taking the floor price—the lowest entry point for a single item in a collection—and multiplying it by the total number of items currently in circulation. This product represents the theoretical value of the entire collection if every item were valued at the floor price. Because floor prices fluctuate rapidly in secondary markets, the market cap updates continuously as trades occur on decentralized exchanges.

## Real-world example

The Bored Ape Yacht Club (BAYC) NFT collection uses market cap to track its standing, calculating the value by multiplying its floor price by its 10,000-piece supply.

## Advantages

- Provides a quick snapshot of project scale
- Enables standardized comparison across asset classes
- Assists in identifying large market players

## Limitations

- Inaccurate for low-liquidity assets
- Floor price does not reflect rarity premiums
- Susceptible to wash trading manipulation

## Common misconceptions

- People often think market cap represents the total amount of money actually invested in a project.
- Some believe that a high market cap guarantees the long-term stability of an asset.
