# Governance Token

A governance token is a digital asset that grants its holder the right to participate in the decision-making process of a decentralized protocol. By holding these tokens, users gain the authority to propose changes, cast votes on key issues, or delegate their voting power to trusted representatives. Governance tokens align the incentives of token holders with the long-term success of the project, as the value of the governance rights is typically tied to the success of the underlying platform.

## Definition

A governance token is a digital asset that grants its holder the right to participate in the decision-making process of a decentralized protocol. By holding these tokens, users gain the authority to propose changes, cast votes on key issues, or delegate their voting power to trusted representatives. Governance tokens align the incentives of token holders with the long-term success of the project, as the value of the governance rights is typically tied to the success of the underlying platform.

## Simple explanation

Governance tokens are like digital voting chips. If you own them, you get a say in how the project is run. The more chips you have, the more weight your vote carries. It is the community's way of giving stakeholders a seat at the table.

## Why it matters

Governance tokens solve the 'bootstrap' problem by giving users a literal stake in the protocol's future. They turn passive users into active participants who are incentivized to help the protocol succeed.

## How it works

Tokens are tracked on the blockchain. When a vote takes place, the protocol checks the user's wallet to see how many tokens they hold. This balance determines the weight of the vote, which is then recorded on-chain or off-chain.

## Real-world example

The AAVE token allows holders to vote on liquidity mining incentives and risk parameters for the Aave lending protocol.

## Advantages

- Direct stake in project
- Aligned incentives for holders
- Easy to track voting weight
- Secondary market liquidity

## Limitations

- Risk of plutocracy
- Token price volatility
- High barrier to entry

## Common misconceptions

- Governance tokens are not just for profit-taking; they serve a specific functional purpose in protocol maintenance. Many confuse them with utility tokens that only provide access to a service.
