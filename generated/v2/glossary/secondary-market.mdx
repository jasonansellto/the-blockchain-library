# Secondary Market

A secondary market in the context of Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) refers to any decentralized or centralized platform where digital assets are traded between users after their initial sale or minting from the original project creator. Unlike the primary market, where the issuer sells the asset directly to the buyer, the secondary market facilitates peer-to-peer exchanges. These platforms rely on smart contracts to execute trades, ensuring transparent ownership transfer and the automatic distribution of secondary sale royalties back to the original creators when programmed into the token's metadata.

## Definition

A secondary market in the context of Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) refers to any decentralized or centralized platform where digital assets are traded between users after their initial sale or minting from the original project creator. Unlike the primary market, where the issuer sells the asset directly to the buyer, the secondary market facilitates peer-to-peer exchanges. These platforms rely on smart contracts to execute trades, ensuring transparent ownership transfer and the automatic distribution of secondary sale royalties back to the original creators when programmed into the token's metadata.

## Simple explanation

Think of the primary market like buying a brand new toy directly from the factory, and the secondary market like a second-hand store or a garage sale. Once someone buys an NFT from the creator, they own it and can decide to sell it to someone else later. The secondary market is simply the digital place, like OpenSea or Blur, where collectors go to buy and sell these used digital items from each other.

## Why it matters

Secondary markets provide essential liquidity for the NFT ecosystem, allowing collectors to exit positions and discover the fair market value of their assets. They also enable a sustainable revenue model for artists through programmed royalty payments, incentivizing long-term creation and community building.

## How it works

When an NFT is listed on a secondary marketplace, the seller signs a transaction authorizing the smart contract to move the asset to a new buyer upon payment. The platform matches the buyer's bid with the seller's ask, ensuring that the token ownership record on the blockchain is updated to reflect the new address. If a royalty is defined in the token standard, the smart contract automatically routes a percentage of the sale price back to the creator's wallet simultaneously.

## Real-world example

OpenSea, Blur, and Magic Eden are prominent platforms where users trade Bored Ape Yacht Club or Azuki NFTs after their initial minting phase.

## Advantages

- Provides liquidity for digital assets
- Enables creator royalties through smart contracts
- Allows price discovery based on market demand

## Limitations

- High market volatility can cause price swings
- Royalty enforcement is often optional on some platforms
- Liquidity can dry up for niche or unpopular collections

## Common misconceptions

- People often think secondary markets are illegal or unauthorized by the project creators.
- Many believe that creators always receive royalties, though this depends on platform enforcement settings.
