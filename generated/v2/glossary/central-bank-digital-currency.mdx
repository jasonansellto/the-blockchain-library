# Central Bank Digital Currency

A Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a country's sovereign currency that is issued and regulated by the nation's central bank. Unlike commercial bank money, which represents a liability of a private institution, a CBDC serves as a direct digital liability of the central bank. While it utilizes cryptographic ledger technology to facilitate secure, instantaneous peer-to-peer transfers, it remains centralized, distinct from decentralized cryptocurrencies, and operates within the parameters of national monetary policy and government oversight.

## Definition

A Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a country's sovereign currency that is issued and regulated by the nation's central bank. Unlike commercial bank money, which represents a liability of a private institution, a CBDC serves as a direct digital liability of the central bank. While it utilizes cryptographic ledger technology to facilitate secure, instantaneous peer-to-peer transfers, it remains centralized, distinct from decentralized cryptocurrencies, and operates within the parameters of national monetary policy and government oversight.

## Simple explanation

Think of a CBDC as a digital version of the physical cash in your wallet, but issued directly by the government instead of a private bank. It is like having a digital token that you can use to pay for things instantly, backed entirely by the government's promise, rather than a debit card that relies on a middleman bank to confirm your funds.

## Why it matters

CBDCs represent a shift in how fiat money is digitized, potentially streamlining global settlement and reducing transaction costs. They matter because they bridge traditional banking with digital technology, influencing how individuals and businesses interact with state-backed money in an increasingly cashless society.

## How it works

A central bank issues digital tokens or entries on a secure, restricted ledger that it controls. Individuals or commercial banks hold accounts directly at the central bank or through authorized intermediaries that interface with this ledger. When a payment is made, the central bank updates its ledger to reflect the movement of funds between digital accounts, providing final settlement without private intermediaries.

## Real-world example

The People's Bank of China's Digital Yuan (e-CNY) pilot program is one of the most prominent real-world implementations of a retail CBDC.

## Advantages

- Increased transaction efficiency and speed
- Lower cross-border payment costs
- Enhanced financial inclusion for unbanked populations
- Direct implementation of monetary policy

## Limitations

- Potential for government surveillance and loss of privacy
- Centralized point of failure risks
- Disintermediation of the traditional commercial banking sector

## Common misconceptions

- People often confuse CBDCs with decentralized cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin, but they are entirely centralized.
- It is a common myth that all CBDCs automatically use blockchain technology, though they often use centralized DLTs.
