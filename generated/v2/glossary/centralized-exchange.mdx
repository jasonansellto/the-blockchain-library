# Centralized Exchange

A centralized exchange (CEX) is a digital platform operated by a third-party entity that facilitates the trading of cryptocurrencies and digital assets. Unlike decentralized exchanges, a CEX acts as a custodial intermediary, holding user funds in its own wallets and managing the order book matching engine. These platforms typically require rigorous Know Your Customer (KYC) verification processes, operate under corporate governance structures, and provide a user-friendly interface that mimics traditional financial stock exchanges to provide liquidity and market accessibility for retail and institutional traders alike.

## Definition

A centralized exchange (CEX) is a digital platform operated by a third-party entity that facilitates the trading of cryptocurrencies and digital assets. Unlike decentralized exchanges, a CEX acts as a custodial intermediary, holding user funds in its own wallets and managing the order book matching engine. These platforms typically require rigorous Know Your Customer (KYC) verification processes, operate under corporate governance structures, and provide a user-friendly interface that mimics traditional financial stock exchanges to provide liquidity and market accessibility for retail and institutional traders alike.

## Simple explanation

Think of a centralized exchange like a traditional bank or a stock brokerage for cryptocurrency. Instead of you holding your own money in a personal wallet, you deposit it into the exchange's digital vault. The company manages all the transactions, protects your account with passwords, and matches your buy or sell orders with other people on their platform. It is much easier to use, but you have to trust them to keep your funds safe since they hold the keys to your assets.

## Why it matters

Centralized exchanges serve as the primary bridge between traditional fiat currencies and the blockchain ecosystem, enabling mass adoption. They provide the necessary liquidity and regulatory compliance that allow institutional investors to enter the market securely.

## How it works

A CEX operates an internal database that tracks user balances, separate from the actual blockchain ledgers. When a user deposits funds, the exchange credits their account in this internal database and moves the actual assets into a centralized custodial wallet. When a trade occurs, the exchange matches buy and sell orders within its proprietary order book. Assets are only moved on-chain when a user initiates a withdrawal to an external wallet address.

## Real-world example

Coinbase, Binance, and Kraken are prominent examples of centralized exchanges that provide services for millions of global users.

## Advantages

- Extremely high liquidity for fast trading
- User-friendly interfaces for beginners
- Easy recovery of lost account passwords
- Integration with traditional fiat banking systems

## Limitations

- Custodial risk of potential exchange hacks
- Requirement for intrusive personal identity verification
- Single point of failure for system outages
- Limited control over self-custodial assets

## Common misconceptions

- Many people mistakenly believe they own their private keys when their assets are stored on a centralized exchange.
- Users often think that trading on a CEX happens directly on the blockchain, when in fact it usually occurs on an internal database.
