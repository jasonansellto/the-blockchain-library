# Distributed Systems

A distributed system is a computing environment where components are located on different networked computers that communicate and coordinate their actions by passing messages. In the context of blockchain, these systems function as a network of independent nodes that maintain a shared, synchronized ledger. Unlike centralized systems, distributed systems have no single point of failure and require consensus mechanisms to ensure that all participants agree on the state of the data, achieving high availability, fault tolerance, and transparency across geographical boundaries.

## Definition

A distributed system is a computing environment where components are located on different networked computers that communicate and coordinate their actions by passing messages. In the context of blockchain, these systems function as a network of independent nodes that maintain a shared, synchronized ledger. Unlike centralized systems, distributed systems have no single point of failure and require consensus mechanisms to ensure that all participants agree on the state of the data, achieving high availability, fault tolerance, and transparency across geographical boundaries.

## Simple explanation

Imagine you have a group project where everyone has their own copy of the notes. Instead of one person being in charge, everyone updates their notes at the same time. If one person loses their notebook, the project isn't ruined because everyone else still has their copies. A distributed system works the same way by spreading information across many computers so that the network keeps running even if some parts stop working.

## Why it matters

Distributed systems are the foundation of blockchain technology, ensuring that no single entity has total control over the network's data. This architecture prevents censorship, minimizes downtime, and creates a secure environment where information can be verified by anyone without relying on a central authority.

## How it works

Participants, known as nodes, maintain a complete copy of the shared ledger or database. When a transaction occurs, nodes broadcast it to their peers, who independently validate the data based on network rules. Through a consensus protocol, nodes reach an agreement on the new state of the ledger, which is then updated by every participating node in the network.

## Real-world example

The Bitcoin network is a global distributed system where thousands of nodes globally validate and record every transaction without a central server or administrator.

## Advantages

- High fault tolerance against localized failures
- Resistance to single-point censorship
- Enhanced data redundancy across multiple locations
- Increased system transparency and auditability

## Limitations

- Complexity in achieving network consensus
- Increased latency due to message propagation
- Difficulty in maintaining consistent data states

## Common misconceptions

- Many people believe distributed systems are the same as decentralized systems, but distribution is a technical deployment while decentralization refers to power.
- It is often assumed that distributed systems are always faster, but they are generally slower than centralized ones due to coordination requirements.
