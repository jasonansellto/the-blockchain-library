# Synthetic Dollar

A synthetic dollar is a digital asset designed to emulate the value of a US dollar through derivatives and hedging strategies rather than holding physical cash reserves. These systems often use long-short positions in cryptocurrency perpetual futures contracts to neutralize price movements, effectively creating a token that tracks the dollar's value. Synthetic dollars offer a way to maintain dollar-pegged stability while remaining native to the blockchain ecosystem without reliance on traditional banking infrastructure.

## Definition

A synthetic dollar is a digital asset designed to emulate the value of a US dollar through derivatives and hedging strategies rather than holding physical cash reserves. These systems often use long-short positions in cryptocurrency perpetual futures contracts to neutralize price movements, effectively creating a token that tracks the dollar's value. Synthetic dollars offer a way to maintain dollar-pegged stability while remaining native to the blockchain ecosystem without reliance on traditional banking infrastructure.

## Simple explanation

Imagine a trader who bets that the price of something will go up while simultaneously betting that the exact same thing will go down. By balancing both bets, they lock in a stable price. A synthetic dollar uses similar financial math to stay flat at one dollar without actually keeping dollars in a bank.

## Why it matters

They allow for massive scalability in stablecoin issuance because they do not require storing physical cash, which is a major bottleneck for traditional stablecoins. This makes them a highly efficient tool for decentralized capital markets.

## How it works

The protocol accepts crypto collateral and uses it to open perpetual future positions. By balancing a mix of long and short positions, the protocol creates a neutral hedge that tracks the US dollar price precisely, regardless of the underlying collateral's market price.

## Real-world example

Ethena (USDe).

## Advantages

- High capital efficiency
- No reliance on traditional bank rails
- Scalable without physical custody

## Limitations

- Complex financial risks involved
- Dependent on derivatives market liquidity
- High reliance on smart contract security

## Common misconceptions

- Many think these are the same as fiat-backed coins. Others assume they are immune to exchange-level risks.
