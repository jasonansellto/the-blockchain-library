# Overcollateralized Stablecoin

An overcollateralized stablecoin is a decentralized currency that is backed by an amount of collateral greater than the value of the stablecoins minted. By locking up more digital assets—such as ETH or other cryptocurrencies—than the value of the stablecoin issued, the system creates a buffer against market volatility. If the value of the collateral drops, the excess margin protects the peg, and liquidators can step in to sell off the collateral to keep the system solvent.

## Definition

An overcollateralized stablecoin is a decentralized currency that is backed by an amount of collateral greater than the value of the stablecoins minted. By locking up more digital assets—such as ETH or other cryptocurrencies—than the value of the stablecoin issued, the system creates a buffer against market volatility. If the value of the collateral drops, the excess margin protects the peg, and liquidators can step in to sell off the collateral to keep the system solvent.

## Simple explanation

If you want to borrow ten dollars, you might have to lock up fifteen dollars worth of Bitcoin as a guarantee. This extra security ensures that even if Bitcoin drops in price, there is still enough money to cover the ten dollars, keeping the system safe.

## Why it matters

These protocols remove the need for a central authority or bank, allowing for stablecoin creation that is entirely trustless. They are the backbone of many decentralized lending protocols, providing stability through math and incentives rather than human oversight.

## How it works

Users interact with a smart contract to deposit crypto collateral, which must exceed the stablecoin debt generated. The contract monitors the collateral-to-debt ratio in real-time. If the ratio falls below a safety threshold, the protocol triggers automatic liquidations to sell the collateral and repay the debt.

## Real-world example

DAI by MakerDAO.

## Advantages

- Decentralized and trustless
- Resilient to market volatility
- High transparency through public ledgers

## Limitations

- Capital efficiency is reduced
- High risk of automated liquidation
- Requires constant collateral management

## Common misconceptions

- Many believe these cannot fail if the market crashes. People think they are as simple to use as fiat-backed coins.
