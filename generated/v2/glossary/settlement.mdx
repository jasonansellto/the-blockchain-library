# Settlement

Settlement in the context of blockchain refers to the point at which a transaction is deemed final and irreversible within the network's distributed ledger. Unlike traditional finance, where settlement is often deferred through intermediary banks and clearing houses, blockchain settlement occurs when a block containing the transaction is successfully mined or validated and appended to the chain. Finality is achieved when the transaction is buried under a sufficient number of subsequent blocks, ensuring that the state update cannot be modified or reversed.

## Definition

Settlement in the context of blockchain refers to the point at which a transaction is deemed final and irreversible within the network's distributed ledger. Unlike traditional finance, where settlement is often deferred through intermediary banks and clearing houses, blockchain settlement occurs when a block containing the transaction is successfully mined or validated and appended to the chain. Finality is achieved when the transaction is buried under a sufficient number of subsequent blocks, ensuring that the state update cannot be modified or reversed.

## Simple explanation

Settlement is the moment a deal is truly done and cannot be undone. In traditional banking, sending money often feels instant, but the real transfer happens days later through a complex web of banks. In a blockchain, settlement happens when your transaction is locked into the ledger by the network, making it permanent. Once settled, no bank or middleman can pull the money back or cancel your record.

## Why it matters

Settlement determines the efficiency and reliability of financial transfers in Web3, eliminating the need for counterparty trust. It reduces systemic risk by removing the multi-day waiting periods inherent in legacy clearing systems.

## How it works

When a transaction is broadcast, it enters the mempool waiting for validation. A miner or validator picks it up and includes it in a new block, which is then added to the chain. As more blocks are built on top of that one, the probability of reversing the transaction approaches zero, achieving probabilistic or absolute finality depending on the consensus mechanism.

## Real-world example

When a user sends stablecoins like USDC on Ethereum, settlement is reached in minutes once the block is confirmed, whereas international bank wires often take three to five business days for final reconciliation.

## Advantages

- Drastically reduces transaction processing time
- Eliminates reliance on third-party intermediaries
- Provides cryptographic proof of ownership transfer

## Limitations

- Irreversibility can lead to unrecoverable user errors
- Settlement finality varies by network congestion
- Requires adequate block depth for security

## Common misconceptions

- Users often confuse transaction broadcast with settlement, though a broadcast transaction is still pending.
- Many believe settlement is instantaneous, but it actually depends on the specific network's block time and consensus rules.
