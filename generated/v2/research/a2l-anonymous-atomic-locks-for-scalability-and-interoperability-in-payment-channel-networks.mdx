# A2L: Anonymous Atomic Locks for Scalability and Interoperability in Payment Channel Networks

## Abstract

This paper presents A2L (Anonymous Atomic Locks), a cryptographic protocol designed to enhance privacy and atomicity in payment channel networks (PCNs). The authors identify that current multi-hop payment protocols often sacrifice user anonymity, as payment paths are easily traceable by intermediaries. A2L introduces a mechanism that allows for atomic cross-chain or multi-hop payments without leaking information about the sender or receiver, while maintaining the atomic nature of the transaction (ensuring that either all steps succeed or none do). By utilizing a combination of novel locking schemes and cryptographic commitments, the researchers provide a robust way to scale networks while preserving user confidentiality. This work is highly significant for the development of privacy-preserving decentralized financial infrastructure, bridging the gap between high-throughput requirements and the necessity of financial data protection in an open, transparent blockchain environment.

**Authors:** Lukas Aumayr, Pedro Moreno-Sanchez, Aniket Kate, Matteo Maffei

**Publication:** IACR ePrint Archive

**Publication date:** 2021-01-01

## Key findings

- Developed a protocol for anonymous and atomic multi-hop payments.
- Addressed privacy leaks inherent in existing PCN routing protocols.
- Enabled confidential cross-chain transactions.
- Maintained atomic properties without compromising network security.
