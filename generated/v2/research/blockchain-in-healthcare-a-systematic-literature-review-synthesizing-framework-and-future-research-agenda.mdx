# Blockchain in healthcare: A systematic literature review, synthesizing framework and future research agenda

This systematic literature review provides a comprehensive synthesis of blockchain applications in healthcare, categorizing existing research into distinct appl

## Abstract

This systematic literature review provides a comprehensive synthesis of blockchain applications in healthcare, categorizing existing research into distinct application domains such as clinical data management, pharmaceutical supply chain, and insurance. The authors perform a rigorous evaluation of the state of the art, mapping current academic discourse to a structured framework that identifies both the technical capabilities and the socio-technical limitations of implementing distributed ledger technology (DLT) in medical settings. By analyzing the landscape of peer-reviewed literature, the study highlights a prevailing trend toward research in data integrity and supply chain transparency. The authors conclude by articulating an evidence-based research agenda that prioritizes scalability, cross-institutional interoperability, and the alignment of DLT architectures with healthcare-specific regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR. This work is essential for researchers aiming to understand the current research trajectory and the gaps that remain between theoretical proposals and large-scale deployment.

**Authors:** M. Agbo, Q. Mahmoud, J. Eklund

**Publication:** Academic Paper

**Publication date:** 2020-01-01

## Key findings

- Existing research is heavily concentrated on data provenance and pharmaceutical supply chain integrity.
- A lack of standardized governance models remains a primary hurdle for widespread blockchain adoption.
- Scalability and regulatory compliance are the most critical challenges identified across studies.
- Systematic synthesis suggests a shift toward hybrid architectures that combine public and private blockchain features.
