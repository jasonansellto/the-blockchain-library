## Abstract

The EigenLayer whitepaper introduces the concept of 'restaking,' a novel primitive that allows Ethereum validators to leverage their already-staked ETH to provide security to additional protocols, referred to as Actively Validated Services (AVSs). By allowing the economic security of Ethereum's consensus layer to be re-hypothecated across multiple external systems, EigenLayer effectively addresses the challenge of 'bootstrapping' security for new blockchain projects. The paper details the mechanism of pooled security, where validators opt-in to stake their assets on secondary services in exchange for additional rewards, while subjecting themselves to additional slashing conditions. The authors discuss the economic and architectural implications, including the potential for increased decentralization of the Ethereum ecosystem by reducing the need for new projects to maintain their own validator sets. However, the paper also addresses the potential risks of re-hypothecation, such as correlation risk and the complexity of managing multi-layer slashing conditions. As a structural evolution in blockchain architecture, EigenLayer represents a shift towards modularizing security, allowing for a more flexible and capital-efficient landscape for the deployment of decentralized applications and infrastructure.

**Authors:** Sreeram Kannan, Calvin Liu, Soubhik Deb

**Publication:** Project Whitepaper

**Publication date:** 2023-01-01

## Key findings

- Restaking allows the economic security of Ethereum to be leveraged by external protocols (AVSs).
- Pooled security significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developers building new distributed systems.
- Validators can earn additional yields by providing security services to multiple protocols simultaneously.
- Re-hypothecation introduces new risk vectors such as correlated slashing and multi-protocol security dependencies.
- EigenLayer facilitates a more modular blockchain architecture by decoupling application security from the base protocol.
