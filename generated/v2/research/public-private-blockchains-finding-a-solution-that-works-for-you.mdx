# Public & Private Blockchains: Finding a Solution that Works for You

## Abstract

This research provides a comparative analysis of public (permissionless) and private (permissioned) blockchain architectures, guiding decision-makers toward the appropriate deployment model for their specific business goals. It decomposes the two models based on key criteria: throughput (scalability), decentralization, security, data privacy, and governance complexity. The paper clarifies that the choice between a public and private network is not merely technical but determined by the entity’s regulatory environment, trust requirements, and intended user base. It highlights that while public blockchains offer maximum censorship resistance and universal participation, private blockchains offer the high transaction speeds, privacy, and regulatory controls required by modern corporate entities. The report includes a decision-making framework for architects evaluating which model—or hybrid combination—best supports their use case. The findings emphasize that the industry is moving toward hybrid models, where private, enterprise-controlled consortia interact with public ledgers for validation and interoperability, suggesting that the divide between public and private is increasingly blurring as technologies evolve to support interoperable cross-chain communication.

**Publication:** Blockchain Research Institute

## Key findings

- The choice of blockchain architecture should be driven by business requirements, not just technical preference.
- Private blockchains excel in high-throughput, privacy-sensitive enterprise environments.
- Public blockchains offer superior trust-minimization and censorship resistance but trade off speed and privacy.
- Hybrid architectures are emerging as a bridge between the benefits of both worlds.
