# Designing a Token Economy: Incentives, Governance, and Tokenomics

## Abstract

This paper presents a design-oriented framework for creating sustainable token economies. Kivilo et al. address the practical requirements for balancing complex systems of incentives, governance mechanisms, and economic parameters. The authors argue that a token economy is a social-technical system that requires interdisciplinary oversight. The methodology draws on systems engineering and behavioral economics to propose a lifecycle approach to tokenomics. The study provides concrete guidelines for developers on how to design incentive loops that encourage long-term participant loyalty while preventing systemic exploitation. Its significance lies in its applied nature, offering a practitioner-focused roadmap that bridges the gap between abstract economic theory and the functional realities of smart contract-enabled incentive design.

**Authors:** Samela Kivilo, Alex Norta, Marie Hattingh, Sowelu Avanzo, Luca Pennella

**Publication:** arXiv preprint

**Publication date:** 2026-01-01

## Key findings

- Token economies require an interdisciplinary design approach involving economics and software engineering.
- Incentive loops must be stress-tested for potential exploit vectors during the design phase.
- Effective governance is the backbone of sustainable token value.
- Token lifecycle management is essential for avoiding premature economic degradation.
