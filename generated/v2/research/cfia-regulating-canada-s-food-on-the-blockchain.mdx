## Abstract

This whitepaper examines a practical application of blockchain by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA). It explores how food safety and regulatory compliance can be improved through end-to-end traceability of supply chains. The paper details the move from fragmented, paper-based records to a unified digital ledger that allows regulators, retailers, and consumers to verify the origin and history of food products. By utilizing blockchain, the CFIA can perform more efficient product recalls, verify food labeling accuracy, and ensure health standards are maintained across the distribution cycle. The document discusses the technical implementation challenges, such as data privacy for producers and the necessity of standardized digital identities for food products. It underscores the transition toward automated regulatory oversight, where compliance is verified in near real-time, reducing the risk of foodborne illnesses and fraud in the marketplace.

**Publication:** Blockchain Research Institute

## Key findings

- Blockchain enables granular end-to-end food traceability.
- Automated oversight reduces the time and scope of product recalls.
- Standardized data is required for effective regulatory adoption.
- Consumer trust is enhanced through transparent verifiable provenance.
