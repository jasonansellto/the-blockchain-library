# Governing & Scaling the Blockchain Revolution

## Abstract

This document addresses the dual challenges of governance and scalability, which are widely recognized as the most critical bottlenecks for the maturation of blockchain technology. The research defines governance in this context as the mechanisms—both technical and social—through which protocols are upgraded and community consensus is reached. It analyzes various governance models, from off-chain social governance in Bitcoin to on-chain, code-based governance in newer decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs). Simultaneously, the report examines the 'blockchain trilemma,' evaluating how different architectures balance decentralization, security, and scalability. It reviews layering solutions (Layer 2s), sidechains, and consensus algorithm innovations as primary pathways to achieving high throughput without compromising security. The findings underscore that neither governance nor scaling is purely a technical problem; both require deep integration with the social incentives of the network participants. The research concludes that the future of blockchain will be defined by the ability to manage modular, interoperable, and self-upgrading systems that can handle enterprise-scale transactional volume while remaining fundamentally decentralized and resistant to capture.

**Publication:** Blockchain Research Institute

## Key findings

- Governance structures must evolve to allow for protocol upgrades without causing chain splits.
- Layer 2 scaling solutions are essential for moving from experimental use cases to enterprise-grade throughput.
- The 'blockchain trilemma' is a fundamental constraint requiring trade-offs based on specific use cases.
- On-chain governance introduces new attack vectors that remain subject to ongoing theoretical debate.
