# Rebuilding Canada for the New Technology Order

## Abstract

This whitepaper examines the strategic necessity for Canada to integrate blockchain technology into its economic and governmental frameworks to maintain competitiveness in the global digital landscape. The study addresses the challenges of current technological obsolescence, arguing that blockchain is not merely an incremental improvement but a foundational shift in how value and data are managed. It identifies key sectors—such as banking, supply chain, and public administration—where DLT implementations could significantly reduce operational friction and administrative costs. The paper explores the geopolitical and regulatory implications of a national digital strategy, highlighting the urgency of fostering a robust domestic innovation ecosystem. By analyzing case studies and regional policies, it suggests that Canadian policymakers must prioritize interoperability, identity management systems, and public-private partnerships to facilitate a seamless transition into a decentralized digital economy. Ultimately, the document serves as a call for a coherent national policy that balances regulatory oversight with the freedom required for technological experimentation.

**Publication:** Blockchain Research Institute

## Key findings

- Blockchain adoption is identified as a critical catalyst for national economic growth and efficiency.
- Legacy system fragmentation poses a significant risk to Canadian industrial competitiveness.
- Public-private collaboration is deemed essential for successful implementation of national digital infrastructure.
- Clear regulatory frameworks are required to mitigate risk while fostering innovation.
