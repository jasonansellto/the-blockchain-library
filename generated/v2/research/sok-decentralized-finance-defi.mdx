# SoK: Decentralized Finance (DeFi)

This Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) provides a rigorous academic foundation for understanding the burgeoning Decentralized Finance (DeFi) ecosystem. The aut

## Abstract

This Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) provides a rigorous academic foundation for understanding the burgeoning Decentralized Finance (DeFi) ecosystem. The authors categorize DeFi protocols based on their functional architecture, distinguishing between decentralized exchanges, lending platforms, derivatives, and stablecoins. By analyzing the structural components of these systems—such as automated market makers, collateralized debt positions, and liquidity pools—the paper establishes a taxonomy that clarifies the composition of the DeFi stack. The study highlights the inherent trade-offs between decentralization, capital efficiency, and user experience, while critically examining the systemic risks introduced by composability, such as contagion risk and oracle failure. This work serves as an essential reference for researchers and practitioners, offering a formal framework to evaluate the security, economic incentive structures, and operational mechanics of decentralized financial primitives. By synthesizing the literature and technical designs of the time, the authors clarify how blockchain technology facilitates trustless financial interactions and discuss the challenges of maintaining stability in an open, composable, and permissionless financial environment.

**Authors:** Sam Werner, Daniel Perez, Lewis Gudgeon, Ariah Klages-Mundt, William J. Knottenbelt

**Publication:** arXiv preprint

**Publication date:** 2021-01-01

## Key findings

- DeFi protocols can be systematically categorized into distinct functional primitives such as trading, lending, and derivative platforms.
- Composability across protocols creates powerful financial synergies but simultaneously introduces systemic risk through potential contagion.
- Automated Market Makers rely heavily on specific liquidity provision incentives to maintain equilibrium.
- Oracle dependence is a critical point of failure that requires robust decentralized price-feed mechanisms.
- The trade-offs between decentralization and efficiency remain a core design constraint for all DeFi architectures.
