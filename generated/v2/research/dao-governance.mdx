## Abstract

This paper provides a rigorous examination of the operational mechanisms and governance structures inherent in Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs). The authors synthesize existing academic literature to delineate the evolving landscape of on-chain decision-making, focusing on the intersection of incentive alignment, tokenomics, and distributed consensus. By analyzing various governance models—from pure token-weighted voting to reputation-based systems—the paper identifies common pitfalls such as voter apathy and plutocratic tendencies. The work serves as a foundational text for understanding how organizational hierarchy is replaced or augmented by code-enforced protocols. It concludes by proposing a framework for evaluating the effectiveness of governance mechanisms in mitigating agency costs and enhancing organizational resilience in trust-minimized environments.

**Authors:** Jungsuk Han, Jongsub Lee, Tao Li

**Publication:** Academic Paper

**Publication date:** 2025-01-01

## Key findings

- DAO governance structures face significant challenges regarding voter participation and centralization.
- Token-weighted voting mechanisms often lead to plutocratic control, undermining decentralization.
- The paper introduces a framework for mapping governance outcomes to incentive design in on-chain protocols.
- Agency costs remain a critical hurdle for DAOs transitioning from experimental to operational maturity.
