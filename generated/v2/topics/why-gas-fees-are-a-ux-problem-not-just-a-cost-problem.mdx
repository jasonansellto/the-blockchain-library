Gas fees are usually discussed in terms of cost.

How cheap they are. How expensive they get. How they compare across networks. Entire ecosystems position themselves around being “low fee” or “high efficiency.”

But cost is only part of the issue.

The real problem with gas fees is how they affect user experience.

* * *

### **Fees Introduce Friction at Every Step**

Every time a user interacts with a blockchain, they encounter a decision.

Is this worth the fee?

That question seems simple, but it creates friction.

Users hesitate. They delay actions. They reconsider interactions that should feel natural. Even small fees can interrupt the flow of using an application.

In traditional systems, most actions feel free or invisible. In blockchain, fees are explicit.

And that changes behavior.

* * *

### **The Problem Isn’t Just High Fees**

High fees are an obvious issue.

But even low fees can create problems if they are:

-   Inconsistent
-   Unclear
-   Difficult to estimate
-   Required too frequently

A system with low but unpredictable fees can feel more frustrating than one with slightly higher but consistent costs.

This ties directly into _[Why Predictable Transaction Costs Are More Important Than Low Fees](https://jasonansell.ca/blockchain/why-flat-fee-blockchains-are-becoming-the-enterprise-standard/)_. Predictability reduces friction. Uncertainty increases it.

From a UX perspective, knowing what to expect matters more than minimizing cost in isolation.

* * *

### **Breaking the Flow of Interaction**

Good user experience is about flow.

Users should be able to move through actions without interruption—clicking, confirming, completing tasks without needing to stop and think at every step.

Gas fees break that flow.

Every interaction becomes:

-   A confirmation step
-   A cost evaluation
-   A potential delay

Instead of feeling like a continuous experience, it feels like a series of transactions.

That distinction matters.

Because users don’t want to manage transactions—they want to use products.

* * *

### **Why Fee Visibility Is a Double-Edged Sword**

One of blockchain’s strengths is transparency.

Users can see exactly what they’re paying for each interaction. There are no hidden charges or bundled costs.

But that visibility also creates friction.

When users are constantly reminded of costs, they become more cautious. They interact less freely. They optimize behavior around minimizing fees rather than maximizing value.

In some cases, this leads to:

-   Reduced engagement
-   Avoidance of smaller interactions
-   Delayed usage during periods of higher fees

Transparency is valuable—but it changes how users behave.

* * *

### **The Impact on Application Design**

Gas fees don’t just affect users—they affect developers.

Applications need to be designed around:

-   Minimizing the number of transactions
-   Reducing unnecessary interactions
-   Combining actions where possible

This adds constraints to product design.

Instead of focusing purely on user experience, developers have to consider cost efficiency at every step.

That trade-off limits what can be built—and how it can be built.

* * *

### **Abstracting Fees Away From the User**

One of the most effective ways to improve UX is to reduce how visible gas fees are.

This doesn’t mean removing them entirely.

It means handling them in a way that doesn’t interrupt the experience.

This can include:

-   Bundling multiple actions into a single transaction
-   Sponsoring fees at the application level
-   Converting fees into predictable, fixed costs
-   Handling fee calculations automatically

This aligns with the direction outlined in _[The Role of Account Abstraction in Making Blockchain Usable](https://jasonansell.ca/web3/web3-without-wallets-the-shift-toward-invisible-infrastructure/)_. When complexity is abstracted away, the experience becomes significantly smoother.

* * *

### **Why This Matters for Adoption**

Most users don’t want to think about fees.

They want to use applications without constant cost calculations.

Until gas fees are integrated into the experience more seamlessly, they will continue to act as a barrier—even when they are low.

Because the issue isn’t just what users pay.

It’s how often they have to think about paying.

* * *

## **WTF does it all mean?**

Gas fees aren’t just a pricing problem.

They’re a design problem.

As long as users are forced to think about costs at every interaction, blockchain will feel different from every other system they use.

The future isn’t about eliminating fees.

It’s about making them disappear into the experience.
